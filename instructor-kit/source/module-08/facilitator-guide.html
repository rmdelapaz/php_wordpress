Session 8: Part 1 Project and Assessment

How to run the last session of Part 1: a studio for the Part 1 project (the Fernhill Community Garden block theme), the timed Part 1 assessment (finishing the Brightwater Swim School classic theme, 60 minutes, alone), and a review that turns both into a map of Modules 1 to 4. How to Use This Guide

  • Session 8 teaches no new lesson. It closes Part 1 with three things: a studio for the Part 1 project (the Fernhill Community Garden block theme, briefed in Session 7, due one week from today), a timed assessment (finishing the Brightwater Swim School classic theme, 60 minutes, alone), and a review that turns both into a map of Part 1.
  • The project and the assessment are deliberately different: the project is the block-theme side (Module 3, with functions.php and the main query from Module 4) over a week, with help; the assessment is the classic-theme side (Module 4, with escaping and hooks from Modules 1 and 2) in one hour, without help. Together they cover Modules 1 to 4.
  • Rubrics, scoring guidance, scoring sheets and the answer notes (the model solutions' real output, and what each common mistake prints) are in final-assessment.html at the kit root (sections "Part 1 Project" and "Part 1 Assessment"). Print the scoring sheets before the session.
  • Every output in this guide was produced on WordPress 7.1.3, PHP 8.5, at the state Lesson 4.3 leaves (pagesco active): what every learner's site is at the start of today. Labs live in labs/module-08/: p1-project/ (Session 7 handed it out), p1-assessment/ (hold it back until 1:25 if you can) and p1-review/.
  • When Part 1 runs on its own, this is the last session: the wrap-up becomes the course close (certificates of completion, what next) and the project hand-in is the last contact. The guide marks where.
The #1 risk of Session 8: the studio eats the assessment

Learners who are behind on the project will ask to keep building through the break. Do not let them: the assessment starts at 1:25 for everyone, and the project has a week left. Say at 0:10: "The studio ends at the break, whatever happens. Your project is due in a week; the assessment is today." Second risk: a learner whose site is broken (the assessment needs a working course site at after:lesson_13). Check every machine during the status round, and keep one spare laptop with a working site, or let the learner pair-run the self-check on yours and hand in code only.

Warm-up questions and answers
  1. Name the five extension points of WordPress and the module that covers each. Hooks (Lesson 2.3), themes (Modules 3 and 4), plugins (Module 5), blocks (Module 6), APIs (Module 7). Part 1 covered the first two.
  2. Block theme or classic theme: where does each keep its templates, and in which language? Block: templates/*.html and parts/*.html, block markup. Classic: *.php in the theme root (index.php, singular.php...), PHP with template tags.
  3. Which three guards does a pre_get_posts callback for the blog home need? Not is_admin(); $query->is_main_query(); $query->is_home() (the query's method, not the global function).
  4. Name four escaping functions and where each goes. esc_html() text, esc_attr() attributes, esc_url() href/src, wp_kses_post() HTML with safe tags (and the _e / __ variants for translated text).
  5. Your project's last self-check line? Each learner's own number: the status round (see the studio triage).
Reading the status round
  • 10 to 12 of 12: on track. They work on R8 (the per-URL check, NOTES.md) and a stretch item in the studio, and help a neighbor.
  • 5 to 9: the usual. Templates and theme.json done, patterns or functions.php missing: Milestones 4 and 5 in the studio.
  • 1 to 4: behind (often theme.json or the parts). First conference in the studio goes to them; the goal today is Milestone 4 started.
  • No number (the theme is not on the machine, or the site is broken): fix the site NOW, before the review: the assessment needs it. wp theme activate pagesco and the self-check of Lesson 4.3's end (wp theme list) first.

The bug walkthrough
  • "Our menu never shows" (T1). register_nav_menus() names the location, wp_nav_menu( theme_location ) and has_nav_menu() ask for it: the names must match, and a typo is silent (Lesson 4.3, Mistake 3).
  • "Something strange when my nephew typed HTML" (T4). The heading printed the search words as HTML. Show the fixed output: <h1 class="search-title">Results for: &lt;b&gt;water&lt;/b&gt;</h1>. esc_html() at the echo, or get_search_query() with its default escaping. Every value from a visitor is untrusted (Lessons 1.3 and 5.3).
  • "Previous and next point to the wrong posts" (T5). The related loop moved the global post. After wp_reset_postdata() Dune's navigation is its own: Staff picks for the season Hello world! (Lesson 4.2, Mistake 2).
Retrospective prompt

"Part 1 took you from a local site to three kinds of theme. Which skill would you now trust yourself with on a client's site, and which would you look up first?" Two or three answers out loud; the rest go in the journal.

Closing

Exit ticket (3 questions, 4 minutes)
  1. A client wants "news three at a time" on a block theme's front page. Where does the code go, and why not a second query in the template? Answer: a pre_get_posts callback in functions.php (guards: not admin, main query, is_home) sets posts_per_page; the Query Loop inherits the main query, so pagination and /page/2/ keep working. A second query would not page, and the main query would still run.
  2. Why does every string in a pattern go through esc_html_e() even though you wrote it? Answer: it makes the text translatable (the text domain) and escapes it at output; a translation is data you did not write.
  3. One skill from Part 1 you will use first, and one you want to review. Answer: the learner's own; read the review answers before Session 9 and pick the refresher topics from them.

If You Run Long or Short

Running long: what to protect and what to cut first
  • Protect: the assessment's 60 minutes, starting by 1:35; the studio triage; the bug walkthrough.
  • Cut first (saves 4m): the predict items in the review.
  • Cut second (saves 6m): the showcase; keep the walkthrough and one retrospective answer.
  • Cut third (saves 5m): the warm-up questions; keep the status round (you need the numbers for the studio).
Running short: what to add
  • Run labs/module-08/p1-review/debug_query_review.php together before the break: one loud bug, three silent ones; the silent ones are what Task 5 is about.
  • In the review: switch Fernhill's style variation to Autumn in the Site Editor on the projector, then show the database copy it made (wp post list --post_type=wp_global_styles) and reset it: rule R8 in one minute.
  • After the hand-in: ask two learners to show their T3 Loop and compare the_permalink() with esc_url( get_permalink() ).
